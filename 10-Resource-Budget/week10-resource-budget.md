## Estimation Approach
[Bottom-Up
To estimate labor costs, the team built the budget from the ground up by mapping individual tasks—like governance, product planning, and team meetings—across the 16-week timeline. For recurring events like 2-week sprints, they used simple math to scale fixed weekly hours (such as 5 hours a week for the Scrum Master to run meetings) across the project. Combining these specific task breakdowns created a precise, predictable labor budget tied directly to planned project work.

1.	Assumption 1 (Product Manager Availability): Assumes the Product Manager is dedicated at 35 hours/week across 14 active weeks to continuously refine the product backlog and coordinate user acceptance criteria with clinic stakeholders.
o	Impact if inaccurate: If the Product Manager is split across other NovaTech projects and can only dedicate 15 hours/week, backlog refinement will stall, leading to Sprint delays and an estimated 2-week schedule slip ($12,000+ in extended PM and Scrum Master overhead costs).
2.	Assumption 2 (Project Sponsor Time Allocation): Assumes the Project Sponsor commits an average of 4 hours/week for governance reviews, steering committee meetings, and decision sign-offs.
o	Impact if inaccurate: If decision-making delays occur due to sponsor unavailability, critical path approvals could delay Sprints, pushing project completion out and increasing overall operational cost.
]
